01Built by hand

It started as a class project. It turned into 300 kits.

I'm Satvik Surapaneni, a high school student in Columbus. Future Spark began as a service project for an English class, where we were asked to find a gap for marginalized students in our community and bridge it.

The gap I kept seeing was this: we ask kids what they want to be, but a kid can decide they're "not an engineering person" without ever building a circuit or designing a bridge. Nobody told me the definition of engineering and made me like it. I liked making things, breaking them, and figuring out why.

So a few of us started designing kits, buying parts, and packing them on the kitchen floor. We deliver them ourselves. We don't expect one kit to turn every kid into an engineer. We want every kid to get to try first.

You don't know whether you like something until you try it. We make sure every kid gets to try.

Satvik Surapaneni, founder

04Opened

Kids decide they're "not an engineering person" before they've built anything.

Interest in science starts early, elementary classrooms get very little of it, and the least-resourced schools get the least. A kit is the cheapest way we know to let a kid try first.

  • 65%

    of scientists surveyed said their interest in science took root before middle school.

    Primary source Maltese & Tai (2010), "Eyeballs in the Fridge: Sources of early interest in science," International Journal of Science Education 32(5). doi:10.1080/09500690902792385

  • 18min/day

    of science in the average K–3 classroom, against 89 minutes of reading and 57 of math. Only 17% of K–3 teachers teach science most days of the week.

    Primary source Banilower et al. (2019), Highlights from the 2018 NSSME+ (National Survey of Science and Mathematics Education, NSF-funded), Horizon Research. Report (PDF)

  • 31%

    of middle-school girls already believe coding and programming jobs are "not for them." By college it is 58%. The decision is made early, before most kids have built anything.

    Primary source Microsoft with Kesar, S. (2018), Closing the STEM Gap: Why STEM classes and careers still lack girls and what we can do about it, survey of 6,000+ girls and young women. Summary

  • 63%

    of Ohio fifth graders scored proficient in science on the 2024–25 state test. Columbus students struggled most in fifth-grade science. [[PLACEHOLDER: the same figure for economically disadvantaged students, from the report card download]]

    Primary source Ohio Department of Education & Workforce, Ohio School Report Cards 2024–2025, state summary. Report card · WOSU coverage, Sep 2026

06Funded

For funders: the case, on one screen.

Everything a small-grant application asks for, in the order it asks. Print this section and it comes out as a one-page sheet.

Problem

Interest in science forms before middle school, yet K–3 classrooms average 18 minutes of science a day against 89 of reading, and only 63% of Ohio fifth graders test proficient in science. Kids in under-resourced Central Ohio schools rarely get to build anything before they decide engineering "isn't for them."

Solution

High school students design and hand-assemble a two-activity STEM kit with all materials and instructions, then deliver it in person, free, through elementary schools, shelters and community organizations. Zero cost and zero paperwork for the partner.

Scale

300 kits delivered to 3 elementary schools and 3 community organizations in Central Ohio (as of Sep 2026), funded by door-to-door fundraising and school events. Goal: 1,000 kits to 10+ schools across Ohio.

Ask

Materials money. $25 makes one kit; $2,500 makes 100. Sponsorship lets us buy in bulk, cut the per-kit cost, and reach schools outside Columbus. Contact: [[PLACEHOLDER: name]], futuresparks0@gmail.com.

Why It Matters

Interest in science starts early, and elementary classrooms spend very little time on it. The kids who could benefit most from building something with their hands are often the ones who never get the chance.

  • The average K–3 classroom spends 18 minutes a day on science, compared with 89 minutes on reading and 57 on math. Only 17% of K–3 teachers teach science most days of the week. (Horizon Research, 2018 National Survey of Science and Mathematics Education)
  • 31% of middle-school girls already believe coding and programming jobs are "not for them." By college, that number is 58%. (Microsoft, Closing the STEM Gap, 2018)
  • Only 63% of Ohio fifth graders scored proficient in science on the 2024–25 state test. (Ohio Department of Education & Workforce, 2024–25 School Report Cards)

How We Count

We keep our numbers simple and honest.

  • Kits delivered are counted at each delivery, by site and date.
  • Schools and organizations are counted once they have received kits.
  • Students reached is the number each partner reports to us when they request kits.

Starting this school year, we are adding one question that kids answer before and after the build: "Would you want to try engineering again?" We will report the results here.
